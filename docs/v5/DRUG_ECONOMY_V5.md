# V5 — fictional substances, NPC autonomy, and underground trade

## Scope and canon

Use the unchanged `Bear_Drugs_Encyclopedia_Edition_1.0_FROZEN.md`. The five established families are Hookleaf/Nictarine, Skunkmoss/Tetrahum, Brewzol, Hiveglass and Nectopiates. Only adult recreational use where approved is legal; cub access remains prohibited in the fictional setting. **Illegal in-world does not mean interaction-disabled.** This is fictional game simulation only, with no actual dosing, production or concealment instructions.

## Player character

- Can acquire and purchase from physically encountered panda, carry, hide, trade, gift, and use applicable items from inventory.
- Use consumes the item and changes health, energy/mood/craving/dependence according to the fiction's different category settings; cues appear in the scene and activity record.
- Mama can notice, confiscate item quantities, and provide one bounded care interaction per day.
- Can offer items in an NPC dialogue-led negotiation. The ordinary item valuation rules still apply; exceptional dependence can distort negotiation.

## NPC cubs

- Have ownership of inventories, ongoing dependence and cravings; some consume items autonomously, display illness, tell Mama or receive help.
- A high craving can make the NPC willing to exchange a personally prized item. Such a trade is not guaranteed: the NPC can refuse and later regret, accuse or seek to recover the object.
- `treasureHistory` records the former owner and important history, instead of converting it into an anonymous generic toy.

## Panda trader and honey

- Adult panda has a unique rendered character, limited stock and honey wallet, and location restricted to Human Trail. He is an illicit contact, not an omnipresent global shop.
- Honey is a **finite, transferable integer quantity** in v5, and may be eaten, causing changes to food/comfort/stickiness. The container/viscosity, hoarding and theft simulation remains incomplete.
- He and the cub do not have unlimited funds, and remote access is rejected.

## Principal balance risks

Craving cannot become a universal exploit to acquire priceless objects; the code has variable refusal and relationship consequences, but needs long-run simulations. Trading should not mint honey or lost sentimental items. Drugs should never be presented as a risk-free universal success buff. Full recovery, parental intervention, NPC trafficking networks, overdose variability, enforcement and aftermath remain substantially incomplete.
